Smokeshow has two kinds of page

There are 34 pages on the site. Every one of them is built from one of two templates. Here is the difference, and the part that makes them feel like one site anyway.

Live page smokeshow.earth/smoke-forecast/missoula-mt/
Reading page smokeshow.earth/guides/how-smoke-forecasts-work/

In a sentence

It tells you about the air right now, in one place.
It explains something, and it is the same for everybody.

What the sky does

It is the reading. It paints the real hour for the real place: pale blue at midday, near black at night. It is supposed to change, and it changes while you watch it.
It is a header. A resting clear midday, a soft sun, the hills. It never changes and it never claims a condition, because it is not measuring anything.

Does it know where you are

Yes. A city page is pinned to its city and never asks.
No, and it has no reason to.

What you can do on it

Scrub back 12 hours and forward 48, flip between the sky and the map, tap a fire, change the place, share it.
Read it, and follow a link to a place you actually care about.

Which pages these are

The homepage and the 25 city pages. 26 in all.
The city directory, the 3 corridor pages, About, the guides hub, each guide, and the 404. 8 in all.

Why it exists

Somebody smelled smoke and wants to know how bad it is and when it clears.
Somebody asked Google a question. This answers it and shows them the live page.

One exception worth knowing. The homepage is a Live page without a place. It boots the same app a city page does, but it does not know where you are, so it asks. A city page already knows and must never ask: somebody who landed on Missoula asked for Missoula.

What makes it one site and not two

Both kinds end in the same place. Scroll far enough down either one and you land on the same cream sheet, with the same hills sitting on the horizon behind it, the same questions laid out the same way, and the same small footer.

The rest of this is for the machines

Notes for a future agent session working on any page this site serves. The canonical version lives in the repo at docs/live-and-reading-pages.md; this is a summary, and the repo wins if they disagree.

Telling them apart in the served HTML
A Live page ships <div id="root"> and the main-*.js entry. A Reading page ships neither, and pulls editorial-*.js (directory, corridors, about) or article-*.js (guides). City pages also ship window.__SMOKESHOW_PLACE__ with lat, lon, label and slug; the homepage does not, which is what makes it ask for a location.
Who writes what
scripts/gen-location-pages.mjs writes the homepage via homePage(), the 25 city pages, the hub, the corridors, About and the 404. scripts/gen-articles.mjs writes /guides/ from content/articles/*.md. npm run pages runs both, and npm run build runs pages first.
Never hand edit these
index.html and public/sitemap.xml are committed but generated. The build rewrites both. Edit homePage(), or HOME_QUESTIONS for the homepage FAQ.
One home per shared piece
Document head, footer, disclaimer, breadcrumb, masthead sky and ridge geometry all live in scripts/lib/page.mjs. FAQ styles, footer styles and ridge fills live in src/styles/shell.css. Live-page-only furniture is in src/styles/seo.css; reading-page-only furniture is in src/styles/article.css.
Matched pairs still kept in step by hand
.map-intro and .guide__header are the same type ramp; .seo-sheet and .guide__sheet are the same surface. They are identical today but not structurally, so changing one means changing the other.
Copy rules that bind every page
No AQI or concentration numbers in copy, level names only. The static payload never asserts a current condition. No em-dashes anywhere the site serves. Canadian pages are metric throughout. Landmarks are hand written against real sightlines or absent. Everything is labelled forecast or model estimate, and the disclaimer ships verbatim.
