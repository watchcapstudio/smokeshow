The app CTA, before it goes live

Smokeshow shipped on the App Store. The forecast page did not notice: it still said "coming soon", its badges were behind a flag that is off in production, and the URL behind the App Store badge was a placeholder that resolves to nothing. Here is every surface that changes, old beside new.

01 · The forecast page

The panel is unchanged. Its last four lines are not.

Everything above the rule below stays exactly as it is: the widget mocks still paint the reader's own sky and still track the timeline as they scrub it. That part was already the best argument on the page. The problem was entirely in what came after it.

What ships today the last line of the panel

Coming soon to iOS, macOS & Android. 14-day trial, $2.99/month.

That is the whole footer. No badge renders at all, because STORE_BADGES_ENABLED reads an env var that is not set in production. Had it been set, the badge pointed at apps.apple.com/app/smokeshow, which is not a listing. So the one surface built to sell the app has been telling every visitor, for the entire launch, that it does not exist yet.

What this replaces it with same slot

Two links, one destination. The button carries the offer, which is what a reader decides on, and it names the city they are already looking at. The badge carries Apple's mark, which is what tells them where the tap lands. The terms move underneath both, where they read as terms rather than as the pitch. Google Play is gone until there is a listing to link to.

Worth deciding now: the button text is fixed. On a clear day it is asking someone with nothing to worry about to pay $2.99/month, and it will convert at roughly nothing whatever it says. On a smoky day the panel already knows the answer to the only question that matters, and could lead with it: "Smoke clears Thursday ~6 PM. Get told when it actually does." The verdict and headline are already passed into this component. That is a small change and probably the largest single lever on the page.

The one that reaches the traffic you actually have.

Nor does anyone on bad cellular during an active smoke event, which is precisely the traffic that converts. Proposed. One meta tag, drawn by Safari itself, above the page, on the first visit, at zero layout cost to us.

Apple draws this, not us, which is why it is worth having: it looks like part of iOS rather than part of a marketing page, it hides itself once the app is installed, and it costs one line in <head>. It renders only in Safari on iOS, so no other browser pays anything for it. This ships on every page the site serves: the app shell, all 25 city pages, the 3 corridor pages, the hub, /about/, and the guides. A test fails the build if any generated page loses it.

The banner shown here is a reproduction. The real one is drawn by Safari from the listing itself, so the app name, seller and price line come from App Store Connect and will match whatever is there, not what is typed above.

04 · Measurement

There was none. Now there is some.

Google Analytics has been loaded on every page this whole time and not a single event was ever fired from the app. Every store link now reports twice: once to GA4 naming the placement, once to Apple as a campaign token on the URL.

Surface GA4 event Apple campaign
CTA button, forecast page app_store_click / forecast-cta-button ct=forecast-cta
CTA badge, forecast page app_store_click / forecast-cta-badge ct=forecast-cta
Return-visit nudge app_store_click / install-nudge ct=install-nudge
Smart App Banner None available Apple attributes it as web referral

Both halves are wanted and neither is sufficient. GA4 says which placement got pressed; App Store Connect says which press became a download. What is still missing is an impression event on the CTA panel, so these become a rate rather than a count. Without it you cannot tell "nobody sees it" from "everybody ignores it", and those two need opposite fixes.

05 · Before this goes live

Two things I need from you, and one I would not ship blind.

Which devices does the listing actually serve?

Every line above says "iPhone and iPad", from a single constant. The repository builds macOS, watchOS and widget targets, and apple/README.md describes one SwiftUI codebase across two platforms. If the listing covers Mac or Watch, that is one word to change and a real selling point currently left on the table. I could not check: the App Store is blocked from this environment, so every fact on this page about price and platform comes from Smokeshow.storekit in the repo, not from the live listing.

The App Store Connect provider token.

PROVIDER_TOKEN in src/lib/appStore.js is empty. It comes from App Store Connect under App Analytics, then Campaigns. Until it is filled in, Apple reports all of this as generic web referral and the ct tokens above are decoration. GA4 will still work either way.

The static store link on the city pages.

Deliberately not in this change. It is the highest-leverage placement left, because it would put the app in the initial HTML of all 30 SEO pages where the React panel cannot reach. But those pages have hard house rules about what may sit beside a place name, and a copy change across every one of them deserves its own pass rather than being smuggled into this one. Say the word and it gets written properly.
